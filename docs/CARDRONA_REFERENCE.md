# Cardrona / Soho Basin reference notes (from user-supplied photos)

Distilled from five reference images provided in-session (real Cardrona NZ /
Soho Basin marketing and drone footage). The photos themselves are not stored
in the repo; this spec is the derived, committable artifact.

## 1. The view — what makes it read "Cardrona"

**The horizon is a WALL.** From the 1700–1860 m crest the view looks *across*
at the Crown Range / Harris Mountains toward Mt Aspiring: four to six
overlapping rows of serrated, deeply fluted snow ranges filling **25–45% of
frame height** in a wide shot. Our current backdrop is a low, smooth band —
nothing like it. Properties per row, near → far:

- Row 1 (8–15 km): warm white faces, visible rock on sun-facing aretes and
  ridgelines (patchy brown-grey, maybe 10–20% of surface), avalanche flutes
  and spur shadows at 100–300 m spacing running *down* the faces. Strong
  shadow modelling — the flute shadows are the dominant texture.
- Rows 2–4 (15–35 km): progressively paler and bluer, but the fluting stays
  legible in every row — aerial perspective compresses value range without
  erasing structure. Crest lines stay crisp against the sky at every depth.
- Sky at the horizon: pale grey-blue; zenith deep blue (bluebird).

**Valley cloud.** Look down-valley and there are cloud banks *below* eye
level, lying in the valleys and lapping the bases of the mid-distance
ranges (inversion). A huge Cardrona signature and free depth cue.

## 2. The basin itself

- Big sweeping planar faces with one hard, curving ridge-shadow edge crossing
  the whole bowl (we have this).
- The crest plateau is littered with **dark schist reefs** — elongated,
  bedding-aligned chains of low ledgy outcrop with drift tails, dozens
  visible at once, tracks weaving between them. NOT isolated stacked-disc
  tors: chains and reefs, long axis shared (regional strike), rising maybe
  1–3 m out of wind-rolled snow.
- Skyline nubs: the crest silhouette shows small dark rock teeth.
- Lift infrastructure: towers and top stations read as small dark rectangles
  on the skyline; at close range everything (towers, cross-arms, sheave
  assemblies) is **rimed solid white** — not clean steel.
- Surface: smooth wind-rolled pillows; old ski tracks everywhere on popular
  lines (fine S-curves, not trenches); mogul texture near lift lines.

## 3. Colour script (visual sample from photos)

- Sunlit snow: near-white, barely warm.
- Shadowed basin snow: strong blue (relative to sunlit), consistent with our
  LAW 2 targets.
- Far-range shadowed flutes: blue-grey, lighter each row.
- Rock: dark grey-brown, cool where shaded, warm-brown highlights in sun.
- Sky: saturated blue zenith → pale grey-blue horizon band.

## 3b. Second photo batch — the snowline changes everything

- **Snowline at ~1300–1400 m.** Below it the country is tussock brown-grey.
  Valley floors and lower slopes are BROWN, seen through and under the cloud
  sheets. An all-white world to the horizon is the single biggest
  "not Cardrona" tell in our current backdrop.
- **The far field has two characters by azimuth**: the serrated white wall
  (Harris Mountains / toward Aspiring) only on the *western* horizon; toward
  the valley/Pisa side the ranges are **rolling and mottled** — thin snow
  over tussock, brown showing through, crests white, faces patchy.
- **Valley cloud**: flat sheets lying at ~snowline in the valleys, banks
  lapping the mid-range bases, and banners streaming off high crests.
- **Crest reef crags**: the top-station crest carries a large craggy schist
  reef (8–12 m relief) integrated INTO the ridge, not free-standing.
- **Cat track**: a groomed road with chunky corduroy tread winds through the
  basin (the SOHO BASIN cat) — a strong mid-ground composition element.

## 4. Implementation queue derived from this

1. **Backdrop range wall** (biggest gap): raise far skyline amplitude and row
   count so wide shots carry the stacked serrated ranges at the reference
   proportions; add per-row fluting (ridge-transverse corrugation whose
   shadows survive the aerial fade) and patchy rock exposure on aretes.
2. **Valley cloud banks** below eye level down-valley (billboard/volume
   banks at ~1200–1500 m altitude in the far valleys).
3. **Crest reefs**: re-shape the tor generator toward elongated strike-
   aligned chains with drift tails; more of them, lower, on the plateau.
4. **Rimed lift hardware**: white ice material on towers/cross-arms.
5. Track-scribble decal field on popular lines (fine old-track texture).

## 5. Soho chairlift video (user upload) — crest rock ground truth

Fourteen seconds from the Soho Basin chair, ending on the demo-v6 view:

- A full crest view carries a HANDFUL of outcrops: one substantial craggy
  cluster near the summit (3-6 m, ledgy, blocky), two or three smaller
  clusters on the upper face, one half-buried elongated reef mid-slope.
- Every outcrop is WIDER THAN TALL, dark grey-brown, heavily snow-draped,
  seated IN the slope with drift merging around its base.
- Nothing vertical, nothing repeated, nothing resembling a tree — the
  basin is fully alpine.
- Confirmed from the down-valley pan: mottled brown-white rolling ranges
  on the Pisa side, tiny serrated white peaks far beyond, real lenticular
  clouds, vast smooth tracked snowfields.
